Vanguard X — Hack the Satellite

Tune in to a satellite. Decode it. Talk your way in.

A five-level radio CTF that runs on the ESP32-S3 “Vanguard” badge.

Vanguard X is the same Vanguard badge as Sysrupt V2, running a different firmware. A second board nearby — the satellite simulator — is broadcasting over LoRa on a fixed schedule, forever. Your badge tunes to the same radio, captures the frames, and works five levels in order: read the protocol, decode the satellite’s identity, reverse its firmware, transmit a valid uplink, and recover a classified payload.

No cloud, no internet, no extra radios. Progress and the level count live on the badge’s own colour LCD and survive a reboot. The badge is also a full event companion — PeerDrop contact-trading, five games, a chiptune player, badge-to-badge radio chat and a hardware self-test — see Features below.

Getting Started

  1. Charge and power on the badge over USB-C, and let it reach the home screen (the drifting Vanguard logo with your Unit number underneath).
  2. Set up your profile once: Settings & Diagnostics → Wi-Fi Setup. The badge raises its own hotspot; scan the on-screen QR (or join the printed SSID) and fill in your name, email, phone and organisation at 192.168.4.1. This is the card PeerDrop hands out.
  3. For the CTF, keep a laptop on a USB-C serial terminal the whole time: 
    # Windows: PuTTY, Serial, the badge's COM port, 115200 8N1
# macOS / Linux:
screen /dev/ttyACM0 115200       # or: picocom -b 115200 /dev/ttyACM0
    Most of the real work — reading what the satellite sent, typing an answer — happens on that terminal, not the tiny screen.
  4. Open Hack the Satellite from the main menu. You land on a count of levels cleared, with a Submit Flag entry underneath it. Start at Level 1 and work in order.

Two helper scripts live on this page for the levels that use a USB transfer: download_firmware_source.py (Level 3, pulls the satellite’s open-source firmware) and download.py (Level 5, pulls the recon-photo payload). Both are plain Python 3 and need pyserial (pip install pyserial).

Nothing in this CTF is solved by guessing. Every level gives you real data; the puzzle is working out what it means. Each level has an on-screen hint ladder that gets more specific each time you ask — use it one step at a time.

Features

Seven items on the main menu — that is the whole badge. Move the highlight with the joystick, OK to open, BACK to climb out. DISP jumps to Settings from anywhere; mash BACK to return to the home screen.

01 · Hack the Satellite

The main event, and what the badge is really built for: a real five-level hacking challenge against a satellite simulator transmitting over LoRa. Covered in full in the challenge guides.

02 · PeerDrop (BLE)

Trade digital business cards by walking up to someone — no typing, no QR. Open PeerDrop on both badges; they find each other over Bluetooth, each side confirms, and both profiles save straight into Contacts. Set up your own profile first (Settings → Wi-Fi Setup). Bluetooth range is short — stay close until both badges say done.

03 · Games

Five games, one shared control scheme (joystick to move, START to begin/restart, PAUSE to pause, BACK to quit): Tetris, Snake, Space Shooter, 2048, and Ship Battle — real Battleship played badge-versus-badge over the same LoRa link the satellite challenges use. One badge hosts and broadcasts; the other joins from a list. Place a Carrier, a Battleship, two Cruisers and a Destroyer on your 8×8 grid, then take turns firing. It is built to shrug off a flaky link — a shot result sent twice is not double-counted.

04 · Music Player

A retro cassette-deck UI, with no audio files stored anywhere: every note is generated live and played through the badge’s buzzer while the reels spin on screen. Six tracks — “He’s a Pirate”, “Hedwig’s Theme”, Super Mario Bros., Star Wars, Mission: Impossible and the Pink Panther. Joystick to browse, OK to play, PAUSE to pause, BACK to eject.

05 · Settings & Diagnostics

Your profile, Sound toggle, Wi-Fi Setup (also how you set the profile), Clear Contacts DB, Reset Challenges (wipes Hack the Satellite progress back to Level 1, no undo), and a hidden Secure Badge row that only appears once you have earned it (see Radio Chat). The Hardware Test Suite is a genuine live self-check of the whole board — the onboard LED and LED strip, a buzzer tone sweep, live battery voltage, and a LoRa radio pass/fail. Press OK inside it for a live Button Tester that lights up each input’s name as you press it.

06 · Contacts Manager

A digital rolodex of everyone you have PeerDropped with — name, organisation, email, phone. Joystick to scroll, OK to open a contact, PAUSE to delete the highlighted one, BACK to leave. Opening an empty list and pressing OK drops in a sample contact so the screen is never bare.

07 · Radio Chat

Badge-to-badge messaging over the same LoRa radio as the satellite challenges. Send one of twelve ready-made Quick Messages to a saved contact (delivery is confirmed), or open Morse (CW): key your own dots and dashes by hand with real timing, tone and LED flash, or fire eight pre-built phrases — a known phrase auto-decodes on the far end, anything else arrives as raw Morse. At the bottom sits Attack: aim it at anyone with Radio Chat or Ship Battle open and, if it lands, their badge glitches, plays an unmistakable buzzer riff and locks its buttons for a few seconds. Land one and your badge unlocks Secure Badge in Settings — turn it on to become immune to incoming Attacks.

Hardware

The “Vanguard” badge is a custom ESP32-S3 board — the same hardware as Sysrupt V2, here running the Hack the Satellite firmware.

SoCESP32-S3, with Wi-Fi + Bluetooth (PeerDrop and Wi-Fi setup)
RadioLoRa — the satellite link, Ship Battle, and Radio Chat all run on it
DisplayColour LCD — every menu, challenge and game
Input5-way joystick + five buttons (S1–S5: Pause, Disp, Start, Back, Ok)
PowerRechargeable battery, USB-C charge/serial (e.g. /dev/ttyACM0)
RecoveryRST / BOOT pair on the back — RST reboots a frozen badge

The badge’s own Unit number (shown on the home screen) is its identity over PeerDrop and Radio Chat.

Tips

  • Lost in a menu? Mash BACK — it always climbs toward the home screen.
  • Need Settings now? DISP jumps there from anywhere, instantly.
  • Frozen badge? The small RST button on the back reboots it.
  • On the satellite challenges, keep a serial terminal open on your laptop the whole time — most of the real thinking happens there, not on the badge screen.
  • Stuck on a level? Use its built-in hint ladder one step at a time rather than jumping to the last hint.
